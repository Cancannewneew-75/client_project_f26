Reflecting and Moving Forward

Finish the Project Responsibly

A final presentation is not the same as a complete handoff. The partner should understand what was created, where it is stored, how to use it, what remains unfinished, and what maintenance may be required.

Closing the project well protects the partner's time and makes the team's work more sustainable. It also creates evidence you can use to explain your own learning and impact.

Handoff Checklist

  • Confirm that the final deliverables match the agreed scope and success criteria.
  • Organize files with clear names and remove duplicate or obsolete drafts.
  • Provide instructions for accessing, using, updating, and maintaining the work.
  • Document important decisions, assumptions, limitations, and unresolved questions.
  • Transfer ownership and access without including personal student accounts or passwords.
  • Review privacy and delete or return sensitive information when required.
  • Demonstrate the deliverable and give the partner time to ask questions.
  • Ask the partner to confirm receipt of the files and instructions.

Evaluate the Work

Project evaluation should include both the quality of the deliverable and the quality of the partnership. Use evidence such as partner feedback, usability findings, project records, and outcomes rather than relying only on personal impressions.

Questions About Impact

  • What changed for the partner or intended audience?
  • Which original objectives were fully, partly, or not yet achieved?
  • What evidence supports our conclusions?
  • What limitations affect how the results should be interpreted?
  • What should a future team continue, change, or avoid?

Reflect on Your Learning

Reflection connects an experience to future action. Describe a specific situation, explain the choices you made, examine the result, and identify what you would do differently next time.

  1. Describe: What happened, who was involved, and what was your responsibility?
  2. Analyze: What assumptions, skills, constraints, or relationships shaped the situation?
  3. Evaluate: What worked, what did not, and what evidence supports that judgment?
  4. Apply: How will this learning change your approach to future work?

Communicate the Experience

Resume

Use a concise accomplishment statement that identifies what you did, how you did it, and why it mattered. Include a result only when you can support it with evidence.

Example: Collaborated with a nonprofit partner and a four-person student team to redesign an information workflow, creating tested documentation that reduced repeated staff questions during project onboarding.

Portfolio

Explain the context, your individual role, the process, key decisions, and the outcome. Protect confidential information and distinguish your contribution from the team's work.

Interview

Prepare a short story about a challenge, the action you took, and the result. Be ready to discuss teamwork, feedback, ethical decisions, and what you learned.

Thank the Partner

Send a specific thank-you message that recognizes the partner's time, expertise, and feedback. Share agreed final materials and contact information, but do not promise future support that you cannot provide.

If the partner permits it, stay connected through an appropriate professional channel and let them know how the experience influenced your learning.
