Starting Your Engaged Learning Experience

Turn Shared Understanding into a Working Plan

The beginning of a project establishes how your team and partner will work together. A strong launch does more than produce a schedule: it creates shared expectations, makes decision-making visible, and gives everyone a respectful way to raise questions or concerns.

Resist the urge to promise a solution during the first meeting. First confirm the problem, the people affected, the desired outcome, and the limits on time, access, data, and technology.

Project Kickoff Checklist

  1. Confirm the purpose. Describe the challenge and the value of the project in language the partner recognizes.
  2. Identify stakeholders. Determine who will use, maintain, approve, or be affected by the final work.
  3. Define the scope. Record the deliverables, important exclusions, success criteria, and constraints.
  4. Assign roles. Name owners for project management, communication, research, design, development, documentation, and quality checks as appropriate.
  5. Agree on communication. Choose meeting frequency, communication channels, response-time expectations, and a process for urgent questions.
  6. Create milestones. Break the project into reviewable stages and include time for partner feedback and revision.
  7. Document decisions. Store notes, files, decisions, and action items where both the team and partner can find them.

Questions for the Partner

  • Who is the primary audience, and what do they need to accomplish?
  • How does the organization address this need today?
  • What would make the final deliverable genuinely useful?
  • Who has authority to approve decisions and final work?
  • What data, systems, brand guidelines, or accessibility requirements must the team follow?
  • What information is private, sensitive, or restricted?
  • Who will maintain the deliverable after the student team leaves?
  • How and when should the team ask for feedback?

Build a Team Agreement

A team agreement makes expectations explicit before deadlines become stressful. Discuss the following topics and write down the group's decisions:

  • Regular meeting times and attendance expectations
  • How work will be assigned and progress will be reported
  • How quickly teammates should respond to messages
  • How the team will make decisions and resolve disagreements
  • How files will be named, organized, backed up, and shared
  • What the team will do when a deadline is at risk

Communicate Progress Clearly

Use short, regular updates so the partner can respond before problems grow. A useful status update includes:

Completed
What the team finished since the last update.
Next
What the team plans to complete before the next update.
Questions
Decisions, information, or access needed from the partner.
Risks
Anything that could affect scope, quality, or timing.

Early Warning Signs

Ask for guidance when the team cannot explain the intended audience, the partner and team expect different deliverables, required information is unavailable, or a milestone repeatedly slips. Revisiting the scope early is responsible project management, not failure.

If a concern involves ethics, privacy, safety, discrimination, or harm to a community, pause the relevant work and contact the course instructional team or Engaged Learning Office.

Keep the Partnership Reciprocal

Community partners contribute expertise, context, and time. Share decisions in accessible language, invite correction, respect boundaries, and avoid extracting information that is not necessary for the project.

As the project develops, keep notes that will help you reflect and complete a responsible handoff.
