Preparing for Your Engaged Learning Experience

Begin Before the Project Begins

Engaged learning places students in complex, unscripted settings where technical decisions affect people, organizations, and communities. Preparation helps a team enter that setting with curiosity, humility, and realistic expectations.

Before proposing a solution, learn about the partner's context, reflect on your own position, identify the team's strengths and gaps, and agree on what a mutually beneficial outcome would look like.

Preparation Goals

  • Develop community awareness: Examine identities, assumptions, privilege, and power. Learn about the community's history and the people who may be affected by the work.
  • Understand expectations: Review program requirements, time commitments, leadership responsibilities, deadlines, and expected outcomes.
  • Assess team readiness: Identify each member's skills, interests, availability, responsibilities, and learning goals.
  • Begin project scoping: Learn about the partner's needs, intended users, constraints, and desired outcomes before choosing a solution.
  • Plan for ethical participation: Decide how the partner and affected community members will participate in decisions.

Questions to Discuss

  • What do I hope to learn from this experience?
  • Who will be affected by the project and its outcomes?
  • What assumptions am I making about the partner, community, or problem?
  • What knowledge and skills does our team already have?
  • What knowledge or perspectives are missing from our team?
  • How will the partner participate in project decisions?
  • What would a mutually beneficial outcome look like?

A Five-Step Preparation Process

  1. Reflect on your position. Identify experiences, identities, assumptions, and areas of privilege that may shape your approach.
  2. Learn about the context. Research the organization, community, project history, stakeholders, and relevant social conditions.
  3. Set learning goals. Record the technical, professional, interpersonal, and ethical skills you want to develop.
  4. Review team capacity. Compare your team's skills and availability with the likely needs of the project.
  5. Identify open questions. List what the team must learn from the partner before confirming the scope, methods, or deliverables.

Preparation Resources

  • Social Identity Snapshot — Reflect on self-awareness and positionality before entering a community context.
  • “Check Yourself” Community Engagement Checklist — Review personal assumptions and ethical readiness.
  • IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation — Understand levels of community involvement and decision-making power.
  • Project Scoping Handout and Terms — Translate partner needs into realistic project goals.
  • Project Management Worksheet — Clarify objectives, roles, and a preliminary timeline with the client.

Before the First Partner Meeting

Bring your questions, not a finished answer. Assign one person to facilitate, one person to take notes, and leave time to confirm what you heard. After the meeting, send a short written recap and ask the partner to correct any misunderstandings.
